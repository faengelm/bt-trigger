User guide · Dry-contact sensor for iPhone Shortcuts

Plug a switch, relay, reed switch or pressure pad into your BT Trigger. When the contact closes, BT Trigger connects to your iPhone over Bluetooth and the Shortcuts app runs your automation.

Like a dry-contact sensor, but for Shortcuts

🏠 A dry-contact sensor for Apple Home

Reports the contact as open or closed to a home hub. You react to it with a Home automation.

📱 BT Trigger

Talks straight to your iPhone over Bluetooth. You react to it with a Shortcuts automation, so it can do anything a Shortcut can: send a message, play a sound, start a timer, run a Home scene, call a web service.

Setting it up

1 · Power it

  1. Plug BT Trigger into a USB power adapter. A phone charger works. There's no battery and no power switch.
  2. Find its Bluetooth name on the label, for example BT Trigger 1.

2 · Pair it once

  1. iPhone: open Settings → Accessibility → Touch → AssistiveTouch and turn it on.
  2. iPhone: open Devices → Bluetooth Devices and tap your BT Trigger's name. There's no code to enter.

On an iPad, skip AssistiveTouch: open Settings → Bluetooth and tap the name there. iPhones only list this kind of accessory under AssistiveTouch.

3 · Make the automation

  1. Open Shortcuts → Automation → +.
  2. Choose Bluetooth, pick your BT Trigger, and choose Is Connected.
  3. Choose Run Immediately, then add the actions you want.

Want something to happen when the contact opens too? Make a second automation with Is Disconnected.

4 · Plug in your contact

  1. Plug your switch, relay or pressure pad into the 3.5mm socket.
  2. Close the contact. Within a few seconds your Shortcut runs.

While BT Trigger is connected, iOS may show an AssistiveTouch pointer on screen. You can hide it in Settings → Accessibility → Touch → AssistiveTouch → Pointer Devices.

Connecting a contact

What you can plug in

Any passive contact with a 3.5mm plug: a switch, a relay contact, a reed switch on a door, a push button, or a chair or bed pressure pad with nothing driving it. Either wire can go to either terminal: polarity doesn't matter.

What not to plug in

No headphones, audio cables, or anything that supplies a voltage. The socket is the same shape as an audio jack, but it isn't one. Applying a voltage can damage BT Trigger.

Using several

Each BT Trigger has its own name (BT Trigger 1, BT Trigger 2, and so on). Pair each one, then make one automation per name, so each contact can run a different Shortcut. Several iPhones can pair with the same BT Trigger, but only one is connected at a time.

Starting over

  1. Unplug BT Trigger and remove the case cover.
  2. Plug it back into USB power and wait a few seconds. Don't hold the button while plugging it in.
  3. Hold the BOOT button on the board for 5 seconds. The blue light flickers very fast while it forgets its phones, then BT Trigger restarts.
  4. Put the cover back on.
  5. On each iPhone or iPad that was paired: find BT Trigger in Bluetooth settings, tap ⓘ → Forget This Device, then pair again.

After a reset, BT Trigger also gets a new Bluetooth address, so phones and iPads that paired before can't reconnect on their own.

Questions

My iPhone doesn't show BT Trigger

Look under Settings → Accessibility → Touch → AssistiveTouch → Devices → Bluetooth Devices, not in Settings → Bluetooth. Make sure BT Trigger is plugged into power, and turn Bluetooth off on any iPad that might be grabbing it first.

Closing the contact does nothing

Check the plug is pushed fully into the 3.5mm socket, and that the automation in Shortcuts is set to Run Immediately. Your iPhone needs Bluetooth on and has to be within range.

How far away can the iPhone be?

About 10 m (30 ft) in the open, less through walls and furniture.

Does it need the internet or an account?

No. BT Trigger only talks to your iPhone over Bluetooth. There's no account, no app and no cloud. Whether your Shortcut uses the internet is up to you.

Can it run on a battery?

It's meant to stay plugged into USB power. It uses about 30–60 mA, which would drain a small battery quickly.

Build your own

BT Trigger is open source. If you'd rather program and wire your own from an ESP32 board, the DIY page has the parts, the wiring and a browser-based installer, and the source is on GitHub.
